School project · 2023

Local heating

Warping is a major hurdle in 3D printing, particularly when working with engineering materials like ABS. This structural defect occurs when a freshly extruded hot layer cools down, contracts, and prematurely warps the cooler layer beneath it — resulting in weak interlayer bonds and compromised mechanical properties.

To solve this industry-wide issue, this project introduces a localized laser heating system retrofitted onto a Prusa i3 printer. By dynamically preheating the exact spot on the lower layer just milliseconds before the nozzle deposits new filament, the system drastically optimizes inter-layer fusion and ensures flawless part geometry.

Hardware strategy

To bypass the physical bulk, ventilation failures, and weight constraints of the laser module, we chose to keep the heavy laser body fixed on the printer frame. The thermal beam is delivered to the print bed via a flexible fiber optic cable, routed through a custom-engineered motorized rotating crown that pivots on a low-clearance ball bearing. This lets the laser point dynamically revolve 360° around the nozzle, staying locked between 5 and 10 mm upstream of the trajectory path at a precise 0.4° resolution.

Software strategy

Because commercial slicing software cannot natively control an auxiliary rotational motor axis, we developed a dedicated Python data pipeline. Our script intercepts the raw G-code exported from the slicer, cleans non-movement commands, and discretizes the trajectory path by injecting new coordinates at standard intervals. It then automatically computes directional vectors and target rotation angles for every triplet of points, recalculates the adjusted extrusion volume variations, and outputs a rewritten, fully synchronized G-code file ready to command the printer via Pronterface.
